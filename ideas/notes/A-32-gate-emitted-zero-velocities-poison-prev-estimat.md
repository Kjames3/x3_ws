# A-32 — Gate-Emitted Zero Velocities Poison `_prev_estimates` and Impose a 0.5 s Ramp on Every Gate Release

> [!note] Candidate — logged, not decided
> No implementation evidence found in the ROI analysis or in code comments.

- **Date Logged:** 2026-08-01 (Hourly Routine Iteration 2)
- **ROI Tier:** **High ROI** (Six-line change, removes a 0.5 s velocity under-report at the single most safety-critical transition)
- **Problem:** Five early-`continue` paths in `_inference_loop` emit a hard `'vx': 0.0, 'vy': 0.0` as a **policy** decision, not a measurement: the $1.8\text{ m}$ proximity gate (`src/velocity_estimator.py:485–496`), the short-history guard (498–509), the estimation-disabled branch (512–523), and the Kinematic Stop-Trigger gate (536–547). Line 602 then stores all of them indiscriminately — `self._prev_estimates = {est['id']: est for est in estimates}` — so Idea 152's acceleration clamp at lines 589–601 treats a policy zero as the track's physical velocity one frame ago and bounds the next report to `max_delta = 3.0 / INFER_HZ = 0.3 m/s` away from it.

  Concretely, for a pedestrian closing at $1.4\text{ m/s}$: at $z = 1.81\text{ m}$ the proximity gate reports $0.0$; at $z = 1.79\text{ m}$ the MLP reports $\approx 1.4\text{ m/s}$, but the clamp sees $\Delta v_x = 1.4 > 0.3$ and outputs $0.3$. The following frames yield $0.6,\ 0.9,\ 1.2,\ 1.4$ — the true value is reached on frame 5, **$0.5\text{ s}$ after entry**, during which the pedestrian closes $0.70\text{ m}$ (from $1.8\text{ m}$ to $1.09\text{ m}$) while the reported closing speed averages $0.75\text{ m/s}$, a **46% under-report** spanning the entire transition. Since $\text{TTC} = d / v_{\text{closing}}$, the TTC handed to `_get_speed_scaling` in `src/ab_comparison_test.py` is inflated $\approx 1.9\times$ and the brake engages roughly half a second late — at the $0.3\text{ m/s}$ nominal drive speed, $0.15\text{ m}$ of extra robot travel straight at the pedestrian. The same $0\to$real ramp fires every time the stop gate trips for one noisy frame and releases, which makes this **strictly worse** once Idea A-29 raises that gate's hit rate from $\sim14\%$ to $>95\%$; the two must land together.
- **Proposed Solution:** Distinguish policy zeros from measurements and refuse to clamp out of one. Tag each early-`continue` estimate (`est['_gated'] = True`) and skip the limiter when the previous frame for that track was tagged:
  ```python
  prev = self._prev_estimates.get(tid)
  if prev is not None and not prev.get('_gated'):
      ...existing max_delta clamp...
  ```
  then `est.pop('_gated', None)` before publishing so the readout payload is unchanged. This does not weaken the limiter's purpose — bounding *physical* acceleration — because a track re-entering the MLP path has no valid physical predecessor to bound against. Complementary to, and not covered by, Idea J-22: J-22 removes *confidence* transients from the clamp but still writes gate-emitted zeros into `_prev_estimates`, so both fixes are required for the limiter to see only physical velocities.
- **Expected Benefit:** Removes a $0.5\text{ s}$, $46\%$-magnitude velocity under-report at the exact moment a pedestrian crosses inward through the proximity gate, recovering $\approx 0.15\text{ m}$ of braking distance per approach event, and prevents the same ramp from re-triggering after every transient stop-gate release. Costs six lines and no additional per-cycle work.
